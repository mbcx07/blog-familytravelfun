The Finger Lakes with Kids: A Waterfall-Chasing Family Adventure

2026-06-12 · Destinations · Tags: familytravelfun, destinations

When most families think of a summer getaway, the Finger Lakes region of New York rarely tops the list—but it absolutely should. Tucked between Syracuse and Rochester, this stunning area of eleven long, narrow lakes offers a perfect blend of outdoor adventure, small-town charm, and surprisingly kid-friendly attractions. My own family stumbled upon it almost by accident, and we've returned three times since. The Finger Lakes are a place where kids can scramble up rocky trails to misty waterfalls, splash in glacial lakes, and eat ice cream while watching sunset over vineyard-covered hills. It's the kind of destination that feels both wild and welcoming, with enough variety to keep everyone from toddlers to teens engaged. In this guide, I'll share our favorite waterfall hikes, lake activities, and family-tested tips to help you plan your own unforgettable Finger Lakes adventure.

Why the Finger Lakes? A Hidden Gem for Families

The Finger Lakes region is often associated with wineries and romantic getaways, but it's also a treasure trove for families. The area is home to more than 100 waterfalls, many of which are accessible via short, stroller-friendly paths or moderate hikes that even young children can handle. The lakes themselves—Cayuga, Seneca, Keuka, and others—offer calm waters perfect for swimming, kayaking, and paddleboarding. And unlike crowded beach towns, the Finger Lakes maintain a laid-back, unpretentious vibe. You'll find family-run farms, quirky museums, and small towns where kids are welcomed with open arms. Plus, the region is surprisingly affordable compared to other East Coast destinations, making it a smart choice for budget-conscious families.

What really sets the Finger Lakes apart is the sheer variety of experiences packed into a relatively small area. One morning you can be hiking through a gorge carved by ancient glaciers; by afternoon, you're floating on a lake; and by evening, you're enjoying a farm-to-table dinner with live music. The region's natural beauty is stunning—think rolling hills, deep blue water, and lush forests—but it's the accessibility that makes it ideal for families. Most attractions are within a 30-minute drive of each other, so you can base yourself in one town and explore without constant packing and unpacking.

Waterfall Chasing: The Best Kid-Friendly Hikes

If there's one activity that defines a Finger Lakes family trip, it's waterfall chasing. The region's state parks are home to some of the most spectacular waterfalls in the Northeast, and many are perfect for little legs. Start with Watkins Glen State Park, the crown jewel of the area. The Gorge Trail is a 1.5-mile path that winds past 19 waterfalls, some cascading right over the trail itself. While the full trail includes over 800 stone steps, you can easily turn around at any point—the first half is manageable for most kids aged 5 and up. For younger children, consider the shorter, paved section near the entrance, which still offers stunning views. Arrive early to avoid crowds, and bring a waterproof jacket because you'll get misted!

Another must-visit is Taughannock Falls State Park, home to a 215-foot waterfall that's actually taller than Niagara Falls. The best part? The main overlook is just a short, flat walk from the parking lot, making it accessible for strollers and toddlers. For a more adventurous family, the Gorge Trail leads you right to the base of the falls—a 1.5-mile hike that's mostly flat but includes some rocky sections. My kids loved feeling the spray and spotting rainbows in the mist. If you're visiting in summer, pack swimsuits because there's a great swimming area at the base of the lake.

For a less crowded experience, head to Robert H. Treman State Park. The Enfield Glen Trail is a 1.5-mile hike that passes by 12 waterfalls, including the dramatic 115-foot Lucifer Falls. The trail is well-maintained but includes some steep sections and stairs, so it's best for older kids or those used to hiking. The payoff is immense—you'll feel like you're in a fairytale. And don't miss the natural swimming pool at the park's lower entrance, which is fed by a waterfall and is a favorite spot for families on hot days.

When planning your waterfall hikes, keep a few tips in mind: wear sturdy, water-resistant shoes (the rocks can be slippery), bring plenty of snacks and water, and always check the park's website for trail closures. Also, consider visiting in the spring or early summer when water flow is at its peak—the falls are truly thunderous then. But even in late summer, the cascades are beautiful, and the swimming holes are refreshing.

Lake Life: Swimming, Boating, and Beach Days

After a morning of hiking, there's nothing better than cooling off in one of the Finger Lakes. The lakes are clean, clear, and surprisingly warm in July and August. Cayuga Lake, the longest of the Finger Lakes, has several public beaches, including Myers Park in Lansing and Stewart Park in Ithaca. Both have gentle slopes, making them ideal for young children. Seneca Lake is a bit deeper and cooler, but its beaches, like the one at Seneca Lake State Park in Geneva, are equally family-friendly. For a more secluded experience, head to Keuka Lake, which is known for its clean water and fewer crowds. The Keuka Lake State Park beach is a hidden gem with a playground and picnic areas.

If your family loves being on the water, consider renting a kayak, canoe, or paddleboard. Many outfitters along the lakes offer hourly rentals, and some even have child-sized life jackets and seats. My kids' favorite memory from our last trip was paddling along the shore of Seneca Lake, spotting herons and turtles. For a more relaxed option, take a scenic boat tour. The Surfing the Finger Lakes company offers family-friendly cruises on Cayuga Lake, complete with narration about the region's history and geology. Or, if you're feeling adventurous, rent a pontoon boat for a few hours and explore the lake at your own pace—just be sure to bring a picnic and plenty of sunscreen.

Don't forget the simple joys of lake life: skipping stones, building sandcastles, and watching the sunset. Many lakeside towns have public docks where you can sit and watch the boats go by. And for a truly unique experience, visit the Cornell Botanic Gardens in Ithaca, which features a stunning lakeside trail and a children's garden with a giant bird's nest and a dinosaur topiary. It's a perfect way to combine nature and play.

Family-Friendly Wineries and Farms: A Treat for Parents and Kids

One of the biggest surprises for us was how family-friendly the Finger Lakes' wineries and farms are. While the region is famous for its Rieslings, many wineries have expansive lawns, live music, and even playgrounds. Glenora Wine Cellars on Seneca Lake has a large grassy area with Adirondack chairs and a bocce court—kids can run around while parents enjoy a tasting. Finger Lakes Cider House in Interlaken is another favorite, offering hard cider for adults and fresh apple juice for kids, plus a huge outdoor space with games and fire pits. Just be sure to check each winery's policy on children; most are welcoming, but some have age restrictions in their tasting rooms.

For a more hands-on experience, visit one of the many u-pick farms in the area. Littletree Orchards in Newfield offers apple picking in the fall, but in summer, you can pick berries and peaches. Schoonmaker's Maple Farm in Penn Yan is a working sugarhouse where kids can see how maple syrup is made and sample fresh syrup on ice cream. And don't miss the Cornell Dairy Bar on the Cornell University campus in Ithaca—it's a working dairy that serves incredible ice cream made from the milk of their own cows. The line can be long, but it's worth it.

If you're visiting in the fall, the Finger Lakes become a harvest paradise. Pumpkin patches, corn mazes, and hayrides abound. But even in summer, you'll find farm stands selling fresh produce, homemade jams, and local cheeses. Plan a picnic lunch from a farm stand and enjoy it at a scenic overlook—it's a simple, delicious way to experience the region.

Where to Stay: From Cabins to Campgrounds

Choosing the right base for your Finger Lakes family trip can make or break your vacation. We've tried everything from hotels to cabins, and here's what we've learned. For families who want convenience and amenities, the Hilton Garden Inn in Ithaca is a solid choice, with an indoor pool and a central location. But for a more immersive experience, consider renting a lakefront cabin or cottage. Sites like Airbnb and VRBO have plenty of options, many with private docks and kayaks included. We once stayed in a cozy cabin on Keuka Lake with a fire pit and a hammock—it was pure magic. The kids spent hours exploring the shoreline while we sipped coffee on the deck.

If you're camping enthusiasts, the state parks offer excellent campgrounds. Watkins Glen State Park has a large campground with sites that can accommodate tents and RVs, and it's just a short walk to the gorge trail. Taughannock Falls State Park also has a campground with a swimming pool and a playground. Camping is a budget-friendly option, but be sure to book well in advance—sites fill up quickly in summer. For a glamping experience, check out Firelight Camps in Ithaca, which offers safari-style tents with real beds and a communal breakfast. It's a great middle ground for families who want to be outdoors without roughing it.

No matter where you stay, think about proximity to the activities you plan to do. The Finger Lakes are spread out, so if you want to explore both Cayuga and Seneca Lakes, consider staying in the middle, like in the town of Watkins Glen or Hector. That way, you're never more than 30 minutes from anything.

Practical Tips for Visiting with Kids

To make your Finger Lakes trip as smooth as possible, here are some practical tips we've gathered over the years:

  • Pack layers and rain gear. The weather can change quickly, especially near the lakes. Even in July, a cool breeze can roll in, and showers are common. Waterproof jackets and quick-dry clothing are essential.
  • Bring water shoes. Many of the best swimming spots have rocky bottoms, and water shoes will save little feet from scrapes and slips.
  • Plan for downtime. With so much to do, it's tempting to pack every day full. But kids (and parents) need breaks. Schedule a lazy afternoon at the beach or a nap time at the cabin.
  • Take advantage of the free attractions. Many of the state parks have a small entrance fee (around $8 per car), but the waterfalls and trails are worth it. Also, the Museum of the Earth in Ithaca has a free day on the first Sunday of each month—a great indoor option if it rains.
  • Don't skip the small towns. Places like Skaneateles, Hammondsport, and Trumansburg are charming and walkable, with ice cream shops, bookstores, and parks. Let your kids pick a souvenir from a local shop—it's a fun way to remember the trip.
  • Be flexible with meal times. Many restaurants in the area are casual and family-friendly, but they can get busy. Consider eating early (5:30 PM) to avoid long waits, or grab takeout and have a picnic at a scenic spot.

One more tip: if you're visiting in the summer, check the schedule for local festivals. The Finger Lakes Wine Festival in July is more for adults, but the Ithaca Festival in June and the Keuka Lake Arts Festival in August are great for families, with live music, crafts, and food trucks.

Conclusion

The Finger Lakes region is a gift to families who love the outdoors but crave a break from the chaos of theme parks and crowded beaches. It's a place where you can slow down, breathe in the fresh air, and reconnect with each other—and with nature. From the thunderous roar of Taughannock Falls to the gentle lapping of waves on a quiet lake, every moment feels like a small adventure. My kids still talk about the time we saw a bald eagle soar over Seneca Lake, and the evening we roasted marshmallows under a canopy of stars. The Finger Lakes may not be the most famous family destination, but that's exactly what makes it special. So pack your hiking boots, your swimsuits, and your sense of wonder—this hidden gem is waiting to be discovered. Your family will thank you.
