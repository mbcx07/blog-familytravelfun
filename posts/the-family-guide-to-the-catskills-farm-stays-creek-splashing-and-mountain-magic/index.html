The Family Guide to the Catskills: Farm Stays, Creek Splashing, and Mountain Magic

2026-05-20 · Destinations · Tags: The, destinations, familytravelfun

When we first told our kids we were heading to the Catskills, they imagined a mountain range straight out of a fairy tale—towering peaks, hidden caves, and maybe a dragon or two. They weren't far off. The Catskills, a sprawling region just two hours north of New York City, feel like a secret kingdom of rolling hills, rushing creeks, and farm-fresh everything. It's the kind of place where kids can trade screens for skipping stones, where the biggest decision of the day is whether to chase butterflies or build a dam in the stream. As a family travel writer, I've seen my share of overhyped destinations, but the Catskills surprised me with its quiet, unpretentious magic. This isn't a place you visit; it's a place you breathe in. And for families, it's a playground that rewards slow exploration and spontaneous detours. Here's how to make the most of a Catskills getaway with kids, from the best farm stays to the creekside trails that will become your children's favorite memories.

Why the Catskills? A Playground for All Ages

Unlike many mountain destinations that cater to extreme adventurers, the Catskills offer a gentle, accessible version of the outdoors. The mountains here are ancient and rounded, with trails that range from easy strolls to challenging climbs—perfect for families with varying stamina. But the real draw for kids is water. The region is laced with creeks, streams, and waterfalls that become natural water parks. On a warm day, you'll find families picnicking beside swimming holes, kids leaping off rocks, and parents lounging on smooth boulders. It's the kind of carefree summer day that feels like a scene from a classic American movie.

Beyond the outdoors, the Catskills have a thriving farm-to-table culture. You'll find u-pick orchards, working farms that welcome visitors, and farmers' markets bursting with local produce. For kids, this means hands-on experiences like feeding goats, gathering eggs, and tasting honey straight from the comb. It's educational, but in the sneaky way that feels like pure fun. Plus, the region is dotted with quirky small towns—Woodstock, Phoenicia, and Saugerties—each with its own personality, vintage shops, and ice cream parlors. There's something for every age, from toddlers to teens, making it a true multigenerational destination.

Planning Your Trip: When to Go and Where to Base

The Catskills shine in every season, but for families, late spring through early fall is the sweet spot. May and June bring wildflowers and rushing waterfalls from snowmelt, while July and August offer warm swimming days. September is spectacular with crisp air, harvest festivals, and colorful foliage. If you're visiting in winter, ski resorts like Belleayre and Hunter Mountain offer tubing and ski lessons for kids, but the focus of this guide is on the warmer months.

When it comes to choosing a home base, consider what your family loves most. For a mix of activities, the town of Phoenicia is central and charming—it's the starting point for the famous Esopus Creek, where you can tube or just wade. Woodstock is more artsy and bustling, with great restaurants and a lively vibe. Saugerties is a bit quieter, with a lovely lighthouse and easy access to the Hudson River. If you want a farm experience, look for an agritourism stay in the rolling hills near Margaretville or Roxbury. I recommend picking one town and sticking to it for a few days, rather than trying to see everything—the Catskills are best savored slowly.

For accommodation, you have plenty of options. Family-friendly resorts like the Emerson Resort & Spa in Mount Tremper have indoor pools and activities, but the real gems are the farm stays. Imagine waking up to the smell of pancakes and the sound of roosters, then spending the morning collecting eggs with your kids. Many farms offer rustic cabins or renovated barns with kitchenettes, so you can cook simple meals. Check out places like the Pines at Sun Lodge or the Catskill Mountain Farmhouse for that authentic experience. If you prefer a traditional hotel, the Holiday Inn in Kingston is a safe bet, but you'll miss the charm.

Top Family Activities: Hikes, Waterfalls, and Farm Fun

Now for the main event—what to do. The Catskills are a treasure chest of family-friendly adventures. Start with a hike that ends at a waterfall. One of our favorites is the Kaaterskill Falls trail, which is short but steep—about half a mile each way. The payoff is a two-tiered, 260-foot waterfall that looks like something from a fantasy novel. For younger kids, the easier route is to view it from the overlook at the top, but if your crew is up for a scramble, the lower pool is a magical spot for a picnic. Another great option is the trail to Vernooy Kill Falls, which is flatter and less crowded, with a series of cascades perfect for wading.

If your kids are water-obsessed, spend a day at the Esopus Creek in Phoenicia. You can rent inner tubes from a local outfitter and float down a gentle stretch—it's a lazy, giggly adventure. For a more active water experience, head to the Ashokan Reservoir, where you can rent kayaks or paddleboards. The calm water and mountain views are stunning. And don't miss the chance to splash in the small swimming holes along Route 28—just look for the cars pulled over, and you'll find a spot.

Farms are a huge draw. At the Kelder's Farm in Kerhonkson, you can pick strawberries in June, blueberries in July, and pumpkins in October. They also have a petting zoo and a giant jumping pillow. Another favorite is the Saunderskill Farm Market in Accord, which has a bakery, a farm stand, and a corn maze in the fall. For a truly immersive experience, book a tour at the Willow Wisp Organic Farm, where kids can help with the harvest and meet the resident pigs. These are the moments that turn a vacation into a lasting memory.

When the weather turns rainy, head to the Hudson River Maritime Museum in Kingston or the Woodstock Artists Association and Museum. The latter often has hands-on art activities for kids. And if you're looking for a bit of indoor fun, the Emerson Resort has a giant kaleidoscope that's mesmerizing for all ages.

Kid-Friendly Eats: From Farm-to-Table to Ice Cream Shops

Eating well in the Catskills is a given, but finding places that welcome kids with open arms and a high chair is key. Start with breakfast at the Phoenicia Diner, a retro spot that serves up fluffy pancakes and eggs benedict. The staff are used to families, and there's a kids' menu with crayons. For lunch, grab sandwiches at the Woodstock Bagel Company or a picnic spread from the Saugerties Farmers' Market (open Sundays).

Dinner is where you can get a bit fancier. The Red Onion in Saugerties is a family-owned restaurant with a warm, rustic vibe and a menu that changes with the seasons. Kids love the mac and cheese, while parents can enjoy a grass-fed burger. Another gem is the Mountain Brauhaus in Gardiner, which serves German comfort food like schnitzel and spaetzle—portions are huge, so you can share. And don't skip dessert: the Ashokan Center's maple ice cream is legendary, or you can hit the Phoenicia Sweet Treats for hand-dipped cones.

For a truly unique dining experience, book a farm dinner at the Hutton Brickyards or the Gaskins, both of which offer family-style meals on weekends. These are a bit pricier, but the kids will be fascinated by eating under string lights in an open field. Just call ahead to confirm they have space for little ones.

Where to Stay: Cozy Cabins and Family Resorts

Your choice of lodging can make or break a family trip. In the Catskills, you want a place that gives kids freedom to roam and parents a chance to relax. Here are a few standout options:

  • Farm Stays: As mentioned, farms like the Pines at Sun Lodge offer cabins with full kitchens, a playground, and daily animal feeding. Kids can run between the barn and the hay bales while you sip coffee on the porch.
  • Resorts with Amenities: The Emerson Resort & Spa has a huge indoor pool, a game room, and organized kids' activities on weekends. It's a safe bet if you want structure.
  • Vacation Rentals: Sites like Airbnb have tons of family-friendly cabins with bunk beds, fire pits, and creek access. Look for listings that mention "kid-friendly" and check for high chairs and pack 'n plays.
  • Camping: For the adventurous, the North-South Lake Campground is a classic—it's the largest in the Catskills, with swimming, hiking, and ranger programs. Just be prepared for the possibility of rain.

Whatever you choose, book early—summer weekends fill up fast. And if you can, stay at least three nights to really unwind.

Insider Tips for a Smooth Trip

After multiple visits, I've learned a few tricks that make the Catskills even better with kids. First, pack water shoes. The creek beds are rocky, and little feet will thank you. Second, bring a picnic lunch every day—there are so many perfect spots, and it saves money. Third, don't overschedule. Leave afternoons free for spontaneous exploring. Fourth, download offline maps because cell service can be spotty in the mountains. Fifth, if you're visiting in summer, check for ticks after any trail walk—it's a simple habit that prevents a lot of worry.

Another tip: embrace the rain. Some of our best memories were made splashing in puddles after a thunderstorm, or cozying up in a cabin with a board game. The Catskills are beautiful in any weather, and the locals are used to it. Finally, talk to the locals—shop owners, farmers, and waitstaff are full of recommendations that aren't in any guidebook. They'll tell you about the secret swimming hole or the best blueberry patch, and that's where the real magic happens.

Conclusion

The Catskills aren't just a destination; they're a reset button for family life. Here, the pressure to entertain falls away, replaced by the simple joy of watching your kids discover a crayfish in a stream or the thrill of roasting marshmallows over a campfire. It's a place where the journey is slow, the food is real, and the memories stick long after the sunburn fades. Whether you're hiking to a waterfall, feeding a goat, or just sitting on a porch watching the clouds roll over the mountains, the Catskills offer a kind of magic that can't be planned—only experienced. So pack your water shoes, leave your schedule at home, and let the mountains do the rest. Your family will come home with dirt under their nails and smiles that last for weeks.
